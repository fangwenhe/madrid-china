Designating China through the Madrid Protocol

If you already hold a home application or registration, an international registration under the Madrid System can extend protection to China in one filing. But the China national phase runs on Chinese substantive standards, and any provisional refusal must be answered in Chinese through a CNIPA-recorded agent. Here are the fees, the process, and when a direct national filing is the better route.

How the China designation proceeds

  1. Home basis — you need a pending or registered mark in your office of origin.
  2. File the international application through your home office; WIPO conducts formal examination and registers it.
  3. WIPO transmits the designation to CNIPA, which applies Chinese substantive standards (distinctiveness, prior rights, Chinese classification items).
  4. Provisional refusal (if any) — CNIPA issues it through WIPO; it must be answered in Chinese by a CNIPA-recorded agent within the stated term.
  5. Statement of grant of protection — once cleared, protection in China has the same effect as a national registration.

Madrid vs direct national filing

Madrid is better when…

You need several countries at once, already have a solid home registration, and value a single administrative renewal. Lower upfront admin for multi-country coverage.

Direct national filing is better when…

China is your key market, you want the fastest, most controllable process, your home basis is new (the "central attack" risk matters), or you need Chinese-character marks and tailored class items.

  • Central attack: if the home basis is refused/cancelled within five years, the international registration — including the China designation — falls, though it can be converted to national applications.
  • Either way, a China-phase refusal, opposition or renewal requires a local Chinese agent.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost to designate China under Madrid?

The China individual fee is CHF 220 for the first class and CHF 110 for each additional class (application or subsequent designation), plus the WIPO basic fee (CHF 653 black-and-white / 903 colour). At renewal (every 10 years) the China individual fee is CHF 441 first class and CHF 220 each additional, plus the CHF 653 renewal basic fee. Because China declares an individual fee, no CHF 100 complementary fee applies. Use the fee calculator for the total.

Do I still need a Chinese agent?

Yes, if CNIPA issues a provisional refusal — the response must be drafted and filed in Chinese by a CNIPA-recorded agent. WIPO only handles the formal/transmittal stage.

What is a provisional refusal and how tight is the deadline?

It is CNIPA's notice that the designation faces an objection (e.g. prior mark, descriptiveness). The response window is 15 calendar days from the date you actually received the WIPO notification (not the Geneva mailing date), and the review must be filed in Chinese by a CNIPA-recorded agent. Missing it cancels the China designation with no reinstatement. Use the 15-day deadline tracker to calculate your exact last day and the CNY 675/750 review fee.

What is the "central attack"?

If the home registration/application underlying the international registration is refused or cancelled within five years, the international registration — including China — is affected. It can be converted into national Chinese applications within the statutory period.
